# Ohm's law and power

For a resistor, the voltage across it is proportional to the current
through it: **V = I·R**. The power it dissipates follows from P = V·I:

| Known | Power |
|---|---|
| V and I | P = V·I |
| I and R | P = I²·R |
| V and R | P = V²/R |

Resistors in **series** add: R = R₁ + R₂. In **parallel** their
conductances add: 1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂, so the total is always less than the
smallest one.

<!-- srl id:tsh116 -->
Q: A 12 V supply drives a 4 Ω resistor. What current flows, and what power
  does the resistor dissipate?
A: I = V/R = 3 A, and P = V·I = 36 W.

<!-- srl id:3tgv3r -->
Q: Why is the resistance of resistors in parallel always less than the
  smallest of them?
A: Each one adds another path for the current: conductances add, so the
  total conductance is larger than any single one.
