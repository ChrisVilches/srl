# Rivers of South America

South America drains mostly east, into the Atlantic: the Andes run along
the west coast and keep almost every large river from reaching the
Pacific.

| River | Length | Mouth |
|---|---|---|
| Amazon | ~6,400 km | Atlantic, Brazil |
| Paraná | ~4,880 km | Río de la Plata |
| Orinoco | ~2,140 km | Atlantic, Venezuela |

The **Amazon** carries more water than the next seven largest rivers
combined, about a fifth of all the fresh water that reaches the oceans.
The **Casiquiare canal** is a natural river that links the Orinoco basin to
the Amazon basin: water can flow from one to the other.

Q: Why do almost no large South American rivers flow into the Pacific?
A: The Andes run along the west coast, so the continent drains east, to the
  Atlantic.
